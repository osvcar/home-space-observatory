Experiment purpose

This field experiment is not part of the main astronomical mission sequence. It belongs to the Home Space Observatory field experiments log: a place for camera tests, physics demonstrations, optical practice and operational learning.

The objective was to practise long exposure photography before the future Polaris camera, tracking systems and signal-monitoring experiments become fully operational.

Newton’s laws in action

The final image looks like a small artificial black hole, but the effect is mainly classical physics. Each spark behaves like a tiny projectile captured during a long exposure.

1st Law — Inertia

Once sparks leave the rotating steel wool, they continue moving forward because of inertia.

2nd Law — Force and acceleration

Gravity pulls the sparks downward, bending their trajectories into visible ballistic arcs.

3rd Law — Action and reaction

The rotating wire, air resistance and released particles interact, shaping the final light pattern.

Safety notice

Fire is beautiful, but it does not forgive.

This experiment involved real fire, hot sparks and burning metal particles. During the session, one spark landed on a foot, burned through the sock and burned the skin.

Always use safety goggles, closed footwear or boots, protective clothing, gloves and a safe open area. Long hair should be tied back, and a cap, hood or protective head covering is strongly recommended.

Water or a fire extinguisher must be immediately available. Never perform this near dry vegetation, buildings, vehicles, animals or people.
