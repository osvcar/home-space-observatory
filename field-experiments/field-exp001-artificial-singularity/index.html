Experiment purpose

This field experiment is not part of the main astronomical mission sequence. It belongs to the Home Space Observatory field experiments archive: a dedicated area for camera tests, physics demonstrations, optical practice and operational learning.

The objective was to practise long exposure photography before the future Polaris camera, tracking systems and signal-monitoring experiments become fully operational.

Newton’s laws in action

The visible spark trajectories are governed by classical mechanics. Each incandescent particle behaves like a tiny projectile released tangentially from the rotating steel wool.

Long exposure photography captures the complete trajectory, transforming thousands of independent particle motions into a continuous light structure.

Chemistry of combustion and oxidation

The glowing sparks are tiny iron particles undergoing rapid oxidation. Steel wool burns because iron reacts with oxygen from the air, releasing heat and light energy.

This is fundamentally the same process as rusting, but occurring extremely quickly due to the high surface area and ignition temperature.

The experiment also demonstrates the fire triangle:

  • Fuel → steel wool (iron)
  • Oxidizer → oxygen from the atmosphere
  • Activation energy → lighter / ignition source

Safety notice

Fire is beautiful, but it does not forgive.

This experiment involved real fire, hot sparks and burning metal particles. During the session, one spark landed on a foot, burned through the sock and burned the skin.

Always use:

  • Safety goggles
  • Closed footwear or boots
  • Protective clothing
  • Gloves
  • Open outdoor areas

Water or a fire extinguisher must always be immediately available.
